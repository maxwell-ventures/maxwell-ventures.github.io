Fine-Tuning a Vision-Language Model, End to End Learning project

A hands-on study of VLM adaptation from scratch — building the data pipeline, running the LoRA training loop, and (the actual point) learning how to measure whether fine-tuning helped at all: the delta against the same model zero-shot. The task — grading building damage in satellite imagery, conditioned on event context — is just a convenient, richly-labeled dataset (xBD) to learn the mechanics on, not a disaster-response product. The deliverable is fluency, and being precise about what the artifact does and doesn't prove. Full write-up in the repo.

Maxwell — Community Pet-Safety App On TestFlight

A React Native (Expo) iOS app for pet safety and community coordination. Point your camera at food and an AI vision model returns a per-category risk assessment (toxic · digestive · choking · allergen); a weather-driven Pet Quality Index folds heat and precip into a plain-language "safe to walk?" read; plus hazard mapping, lost-dog beacons, and "pack" chat. Real production concerns throughout — Hermes, crash-demotion to soft exceptions, Sentry observability, in-flight request de-dup and cache-safe forecasting.

NOM NOM TOKENS — One-Input Arcade Game Playable

A mindless, one-input arcade game about an AI chat-bubble that eats tokens until its context window overflows — Pac-Man's endless chomp, Flappy Bird's one-input lane, wrapped in dry corporate-AI satire. The joke is encoded in the physics: resting is fatal. Pure HTML5 canvas + vanilla ES modules, no build step; game logic fully decoupled from rendering so the same core wraps into an iOS app via Capacitor.

03About

I build things to understand them. The through-line across my work isn't a single stack or domain — it's curiosity. If a problem is interesting, I'd rather stand up a working prototype and learn where the hard parts actually are than theorize about them.

That's taken me from fine-tuning vision-language models on satellite disaster imagery, to shipping a community pet-safety app to TestFlight, to building tools, games, and MCP servers in between. I care about the parts most demos skip: measuring whether the clever idea actually helped, and writing down honestly what it does — and doesn't — prove.
